## Why the Rat Lab Portal Comic Still Matters

Few pieces of tie-in content in gaming carry as much narrative weight as *Portal 2: Lab Rat*. This free digital comic, published in two parts in April 2011, exists to explain exactly what happened between the ending of *Portal* and the opening of *Portal 2*. Without it, Chell simply wakes up in a decaying Aperture Science facility with no explanation, and the sequel's first hour feels like an unexplained jump cut. The Rat Lab portal comic closes that gap in just 26 pages.

For lore hunters, this is required reading rather than optional flavor text. It turns Doug Rattmann — "the Ratman," whose scribbled dens and murals players already noticed in *Portal* — into a real character with a motive. It also explains why Chell ended up at the very top of the test subject list, how GLaDOS obtained her neurotoxin, and where the Morality Core came from. Understanding those answers makes the Rat Lab portal story feel less like marketing and more like a missing chapter that was always meant to be there.

It was the first official comic set in the shared *Half-Life* and *Portal* universe, which is part of why it gets so much attention from fans. It was also distributed free, and it was later collected in print by Dark Horse in *The Sacrifice and Other Steam-Powered Stories*.

## Inside the Story of the Rat Lab Portal Comic

The comic splits cleanly into two halves released three days apart, and each half alternates between present-day action and flashbacks. That structure matters: the present explains what Rattmann is doing, while the flashbacks explain why Aperture Science collapsed the way it did.

### Part 1: Final Pills and a Failed Rescue

Rattmann has clearly stopped taking his medication, and he swallows his last two pills despite the Companion Cube urging him not to. Between flashbacks, he races through the Enrichment Center leaving murals behind him while Chell reaches Test Chamber 19 and destroys GLaDOS. When Rattmann surfaces hoping to find her free, he instead watches the Party Escort Bot drag her back inside. A flashback shows Henry presenting the newly built Morality Core, designed to suppress GLaDOS' urge to kill. Despite the Cube telling him to run, Rattmann goes back in, believing the disaster is his responsibility.

### Part 2: Turrets, Cryo-Chambers, and the File Room

As his medication kicks in, the Cube fades into silence — a detail community reports widely interpret as the Cube being a hallucination tied to his condition. Rattmann finds Chell sealed in a Cryo-Chamber and heads for Cryo-Control, but Sentry Turrets block the path. He discovers that GLaDOS' destruction blew the main power grid, taking every Cryo-Chamber offline. He tries to sprint past the turrets and is shot down.

The final pages carry the heaviest implication: Chell's paperwork states she should never have been tested because she is abnormally stubborn and never gives up. That stubbornness is precisely why she survives everything that follows.

Rattmann carries the emotional load. He is not a hero in the traditional sense; he is a medicated, isolated survivor who decides that one person deserves a chance. That framing is what makes the Rat Lab portal comic land as a character study instead of a lore dump.

## How the Rat Lab Portal Comic Connects the Two Games

The cleverest thing about the comic is that it retroactively explains mechanics and set pieces players already experienced. Nothing feels bolted on, because most of the answers were hiding in plain sight in *Portal*.

| In-Game Detail | Explanation From the Comic |
|---|---|
| Chell is an unexplained test subject | Rattmann finds her file and moves her to the top of the test subject list |
| GLaDOS has a Morality Core | Henry shows Rattmann the core built to curb her killing instinct |
| GLaDOS has access to neurotoxin | Henry approves "a little neurotoxin" for a Bring Your Cat to Work Day experiment |
| Chell wakes up years later in a ruined facility | The Party Escort Bot dragged her back; Cryo-Chambers went offline after the power grid blew |
| Rattmann's dens, murals, and scribbles | Created as he moved through the facility between the two games |
| Portal 2's overgrown, damaged test chambers | Result of GLaDOS' destruction and the subsequent power failure |

If you want to see how those threads pay off in the sequel, the [Portal 2 store page on Steam](https://store.steampowered.com/app/620/Portal_2/) is the easiest place to check the game's current availability and system requirements before a replay.

A practical reading order that works for most people:

- **First playthrough:** finish *Portal*, read *Lab Rat*, then start *Portal 2* — the opening hits much harder.
- **Replay route:** finish both games, then read the comic so the flashbacks click into the timeline you already know.
- **Lore deep dive:** read it panel by panel alongside the *Portal* ARG material, since Rattmann appears indirectly there too.

A few tips for getting the most out of a read-through:

- **Slow down on the panels without dialogue.** The environmental storytelling, like dead employees and ruined offices, does a lot of quiet work.
- **Track the pill timeline.** Rattmann's medication is the clock the whole story runs on, and the Cube's fading voice marks each stage.
- **Re-read after finishing *Portal 2*.** The neurotoxin flashback reads very differently once you have seen GLaDOS in full.
- **Ignore any claim that the comic changes the ending.** It expands context; it does not rewrite what happens in either game.

## FAQ

**What is the Rat Lab portal comic about?**
It follows Doug Rattmann, an Aperture Science employee, in the period between *Portal* and *Portal 2*. He moves Chell's test file to the top of the list, watches her destroy GLaDOS, and then tries and fails to rescue her before the facility's Cryo-Chambers go offline.

**Do I need to read it before playing Portal 2?**
You do not have to, but the sequel's opening makes far more sense if you do. The comic explains the time gap, the ruined facility, and why Chell is still inside Aperture Science at all.

**How long does it take to read, and where can I find it?**
The comic runs 26 pages across two parts, so it takes most readers 20 to 30 minutes. It was released free online, and a collected print edition exists through Dark Horse.

**Who is Doug Rattmann, and why is he called the Ratman?**
He is the Aperture employee whose hidden dens and wall murals appear throughout *Portal*. The nickname comes from those hiding spots, and the comic gives him his first formal appearance as a full character rather than a background detail.

**Is the comic considered canon?**
Yes. It was produced in-house at Valve with writers from the *Portal 2* team, and it is treated as official story material that bridges the two games.
