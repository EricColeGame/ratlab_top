The Rat Lab badge is more than a profile decoration — it is a five-tier collectible that gives a short indie game genuine long-tail replay value. If you have already rolled credits and want a reason to keep opening the game, completing the set is the cheapest, most structured goal available. This guide walks through every Series 1 card, the real cost of chasing the foil Rat Lab badge, the XP each tier is worth, and practical tips for finishing the collection without overspending.

Rat Lab is the indie title from developer Chump Squad, published by Klei Publishing and released on April 15, 2025. Like many games in the Klei publishing orbit, it shipped with a full Steam collectible suite: five trading cards, five badge tiers, one foil badge, eight emoticons, and three profile backgrounds. Everything below reflects the Series 1 set as tracked on the [official Steam page for Rat Lab](https://store.steampowered.com/app/1304610/) and community card trackers, with prices being snapshots rather than fixed values.

The Block Party foil alone can cost more than fifty dollars, and the Touch Grass foil frequently has no active listing at all. That is a common pattern on small indie sets: one or two foils effectively vanish from the market because holders never sell.

Player experience suggests two sensible approaches. First, if you simply want a foil Rat Lab badge on your profile, wait for a booster pack drop and hope for foils — mathematically unlikely, but free. Second, if you are buying, set a maximum budget and treat the badge as a long-term trade project rather than a purchase. Community reports also note that foil prices on low-volume sets swing hard when a single seller undercuts, so patience genuinely pays.

## Badge Levels, XP, and Crafting Steps

Crafting a badge consumes a full set of five cards and awards XP toward your Steam profile level. Rat Lab's five tiers each carry a different XP value in the tracker listings.

| Foil Level 1 | acceptable.png | 100 | 5 foils |

The naming here is a running joke about corporate life — "minimum viable" and "above minimum viable" as badge ranks is exactly the kind of dry humor the game's audience enjoys. Crafting all five tiers means buying or trading 25 cards in total.

**Step-by-step: how to craft your first Rat Lab badge**

1. Launch Rat Lab and let it run long enough to receive your playtime card drops. Most Steam sets drop roughly half the total, so expect two or three free cards.
2. Open your Steam inventory and confirm which cards you already own.
3. Buy the missing commons on the Steam Community Market, or trade duplicates with friends who own the game.
4. Open your badge page from your profile, select Rat Lab, and click **Craft Badge**.
5. Repeat with a fresh set to advance to Level 2 and beyond.

If your goal is purely profile level, note that repeating a cheap badge can be more efficient than chasing a single expensive one. Rat Lab's low per-craft cost makes it an attractive "filler" badge for level grinders.

A few final tips from the community. Watch for booster pack drops from the Series 1 booster — selling the pack is often more profitable than opening it, since three random cards rarely outperform the pack price. Also keep an eye on the game's store page during sales; more active players means more cards entering circulation and slightly softer prices.

For anyone who simply likes the game, the honest answer is that the badge is a nice finishing touch. For anyone who cares about Steam level, it is a cheap, repeatable craft. Neither group will regret the purchase.

## FAQ

**How many cards do I need for a Rat Lab badge?**
Five trading cards make one craft at Level 1. Because Rat Lab has five badge tiers, reaching the maximum level takes 25 cards in total, while the separate foil badge requires five foil cards.

**Does Rat Lab have a foil badge?**
Yes. There is one foil badge, listed at Level 1 with the badge name "acceptable.png." It requires a complete set of five foil cards, which is significantly more expensive than the common set.

**Can I earn the Rat Lab badge for free?**
Partially. Cards drop from playtime, usually covering roughly half the set, but finishing a craft almost always means trading or buying the rest. Emoticons and backgrounds come from crafting and from booster packs rather than direct drops.

**Is the Rat Lab badge still obtainable in 2026?**
Yes. The collectible set remains live and cards continue to circulate on the Steam Community Market, so new players can still craft every tier, including the foil version, as long as listings are available.
